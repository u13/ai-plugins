# Readiness

The idea is ready for planning when ALL of these hold:

1. Every area below that applies to the project's purpose is filled with agreed content. The others are marked "not applicable" with a reason.
2. No open questions. No `assumed` items. No contradictions.
3. Every `parked` item states the consequence of leaving it open and a trigger to revisit it.
4. The user said yes to the final summary.

## Areas
1. **Problem** - what pain or opportunity, for whom, what happens today without it.
2. **Users** - who they are, in what situation they use it.
3. **Solution** - what it does, the core flow in a few steps, the one thing it must do well.
4. **Scope** - in, out, later. Out is as important as in.
5. **Success criteria** - testable statements: how we will know it works, for the user and for the owner.
6. **Constraints** - time, budget, platforms, tech or team limits, existing systems, legal and privacy.
7. **External services and credentials** - accounts, APIs, data sources; which need a real key or paid call, and at which step.
8. **Alternatives** - at least one real alternative, including "do nothing", "simpler first version", "reuse or buy".
9. **Risks and unknowns** - what could make it fail, with a mitigation or an owner of the question.
10. **Decisions** - each with a status and, for non-obvious ones, the reason.
11. **First slice** - the smallest useful first release or milestone, as a hint for the planner.
12. **Demand and market** - who needs this and how badly, what they use today, competitors and substitutes, evidence that people want it (not only that the idea sounds good).
13. **Business model and costs** - how it could earn (options with pros and cons), what it costs to build and to run (hosting, APIs, support, time), where it breaks even. Pricing is the user's decision.
14. **Experience** - UX and UI: the core flow, key screens and states (empty, loading, error), friction, onboarding. Psychology: why a person starts, trusts, comes back, and what makes them hesitate or leave.
15. **Reaching users** - channels, the first users and how to get them, discovery (SEO, communities, partnerships), the message. Positioning is the user's decision.

For ideas that are not software (content, a service, an offer), read areas 6, 7 and 11 loosely: constraints and dependencies of any kind, and the first useful step.

## Which areas apply
Ask the project's purpose in round 1 (unless the user already said it). It decides how deep areas 12-15 go:

| Purpose | 12 Demand | 13 Business and costs | 14 Experience | 15 Reaching users |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial product or service | required | required | required | required |
| Internal tool | who uses it and what they do today | costs and time only | required | rollout and adoption in the team |
| Hobby or learning | not applicable | not applicable | as needed | not applicable |
| Open source | who it helps, existing projects | maintenance cost and sustainability | as needed | how people find it |

"Not applicable" is allowed only with a one-line reason the user has seen.

## Evidence rule (areas 12-15)
Market claims are where an AI invents things most easily. So:
- The user's own knowledge of the market comes first; ask before researching.
- Any claim about demand, competitors, prices or market size has a source (URL and date) or is labelled `unverified`.
- Never state a number you cannot source. Say "unknown" and offer a way to find out.
- Competitor prices and features come from their own pages, not from memory.
- Findings are `proposed` until the user confirms them.

## Question quality
- Ask only what changes what gets built or how success is judged.
- Look it up first (project files, the web) instead of asking.
- One batch per round, most impactful first, never more than 7.
- Each question: why it matters, options, a recommendation.
- Plain words, no jargon the user did not use. Do not lead the answer.

## Stopping
Never loop silently. After round 3 without readiness, name the blockers and let the user choose between continuing, parking with stated consequences, or stopping. A brief with parked items can still be ready if the user accepts them knowingly.
